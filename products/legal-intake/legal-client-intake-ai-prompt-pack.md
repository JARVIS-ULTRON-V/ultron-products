# Legal Client Intake AI Prompt Pack

**Firm-grade prompts for intake, drafting, research and client comms**

A practical, ready-to-use collection of 52 prompts and 8 templates designed for solo practitioners, paralegals, and small law firms. This pack helps you run a cleaner intake, capture better matter information, draft faster, and keep clients informed — without ever letting the AI outrank your judgment.

---

## Responsible Use & Verification (Please Read First)

This pack is a productivity aid for licensed legal professionals. It is **not a substitute for legal advice, legal judgment, or the professional responsibilities you owe your client, your jurisdiction, and the court.**

Before you use anything in this document, commit to the following ground rules:

1. **AI drafts; you approve.** Every prompt in this pack is engineered to produce a *draft* that you must review, correct, and approve before it goes anywhere. The AI is a starting point, never the final word.
2. **Verify all citations, statutes, and factual assertions.** Language models can and do hallucinate — inventing case names, mis-citing statutes, and producing confident-sounding but wrong legal propositions. You must independently verify every citation against primary sources (the official reporter, the jurisdiction's codified statutes, court rules) before relying on it.
3. **Check the jurisdiction's current rules.** Law changes. A statute, rule, or form that was correct last year may have been amended, superseded, or invalidated. Confirm that any rule you rely on is current in your specific jurisdiction, court, and venue.
4. **Do not paste client confidential information into consumer AI tools.** The AI tool you use may train on your inputs, store them, or share them with third parties. Before feeding in any client name, facts, or documents, check your firm's data policy, your confidentiality obligations, and the tool's terms — and use a professional-grade, confidential tool (or a sandboxed/de-identified version) where appropriate.
5. **Time-sensitive matters need current verification.** Deadlines, court dates, and filing rules are live, high-stakes facts. Never rely on AI output for a deadline or date; confirm against the docket, the court's calendar, and your own case management system.
6. **Conflicts and privilege are yours to manage.** The AI does not know your firm's conflicts database or your client's posture. Run every proper conflict check through your own system, and treat all communications with clients as potentially privileged material that must remain protected.
7. **Follow your jurisdiction's ethics rules on technology use.** Many jurisdictions require lawyers to remain competent in technology and to supervise any AI assistance. Ensure your use of these tools complies with your applicable rules of professional conduct, including any disclosure obligations to clients about your use of AI.

> **The one-line rule:** Treat everything the AI produces as a *draft to be checked*, not an answer. When in doubt, verify. When you cannot verify, do not use.

## How to Use This Pack

1. **Pick a prompt.** Each prompt below lives under a category heading and has a descriptive name. Uses the one that matches the task you are doing.
2. **Fill in the placeholders.** Every prompt uses `{{double-brace placeholders}}`. Replace each placeholder with your real details — client names, jurisdiction, dates, facts.
3. **Paste into your AI tool.** Use the same AI tool you normally use. For anything involving client facts, apply the confidentiality rules above.
4. **Get a draft, then verify.** The prompt tells the AI what to produce. Review the output, check every citation and assertion, fix what is wrong, and make it yours.
5. **Only then use it.** The draft becomes a usable work product once you — the responsible professional — have verified and approved it.
6. **Use the templates.** The eight intake and matter-management templates in the next section are ready to paste into your documents, CRM, or case management system. They give your practice a consistent structure.
7. **Run the verification checklist.** Before relying on anything, walk the checklist in the Verification section at the end of this pack.

## Category 1: Client Intake & Intake Notes

### Prompt 1.1 — Draft a Client Intake Interview Script

The AI drafts a conversational script you can use live with a new client.

```
You are a legal intake interviewing assistant for a {{practice area}} firm. Draft a structured intake interview script for an intake call with a prospective client about a {{matter type}} matter.

The script should:
1. Open with a warm, professional greeting and set the call's purpose.
2. Ask for basic identifying details (name, contact info, how they found the firm).
3. Prompt the client to describe their legal issue in their own words.
4. Ask clarifying follow-up questions to surface key facts: parties involved, timeline, documents in hand, deadlines, and desired outcome.
5. Ask about prior attorneys, prior/current litigation, and any other professionals involved.
6. Wrap up with next steps, what the client should gather, and a request for permission to proceed with a conflict check.

Write the script as a labeled dialogue with interviewer lines and brief notes on what to listen for. Keep each question open-ended where possible.

Use placeholders like {{client name}} where real client details will go. Output only the script, with no preamble.
```

**How to use:** Practice the script before a live call and record the client's actual answers into your intake form. *Verification step:* confirm any facts the client gives are logged accurately and verified against documents before you rely on them.

### Prompt 1.2 — Create an Intake Summary From Raw Notes

**How to use:** Paste your notes in, get a clean summary, then review the for accuracy. *Verification step:* compare the summary line-by-line against your original notes and the client's confirmed facts; fix anything missing or misstated before it enters the file.

## Verification Checklist

Before you rely on any AI-generated output, walk this checklist. If an item is unchecked, treat the output as unverified until you resolve it.

| What to check | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Citations** | AI can hallucinate case names, reporters, and pincites. | Verify every citation against the official reporter or a trusted legal database. |
| **Statutes & rules** | Statutes get amended; AI memory goes stale. | Check the current, amended text in your jurisdiction's official code and court rules. |
| **Jurisdiction** | Law differs by state, court, and venue. | Confirm the rule applies in your exact jurisdiction and venue. |
| **Dates & deadlines** | Deadlines are live, high-stakes facts. | Confirm against the docket, court calendar, and your case management system. |
| **Client facts** | AI may misremember or embellish details you gave it. | Recheck every factual assertion against the file and the client's own materials. |
| **Tone & audience** | A draft may be too aggressive, too soft, or too legalistic. | Read it as the reader would; adjust to the client, opposing counsel, or court. |
| **Confidentiality & privilege** | Pasting client data into an AI tool can breach confidentiality. | Check your firm's data policy and the tool's terms before inputting anything. |
| **Applicability of cases** | A cited case may be distinguishable or overruled. | Confirm the case is still good law and on point (Shepardize/KeyCite). |
| **Contract/court-form compliance** | Courts and deals have specific formatting and content rules. | Check against the court's local rules or the deal's requirements. |
| **Logical completeness** | Drafts often skip a step or a counterargument. | Check that the document addresses all elements and objections. |
| **The human review** | No AI output is "final." | A licensed attorney must review, correct, and approve before anything is used or sent. |
